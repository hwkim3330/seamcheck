## What is your contribution?
```
seamcheck: automatic detection AND repair of sheet switches in traced surfaces
(Open Problem #3), with a corpus-wide control. No GPU, no CT volume, no model weights.

(1) Scroll data: every traced surface in the open dataset. 45 scrolls, 322 segments,
1,246 tifxyz representations scanned. Repair was run on all 442 representations of
the 96 flagged segments, plus 185 representations of 40 random clean segments as a
control. Showcase: PHercParis4, 20230702185753_v14.

(2) Why it raises the odds of reading: a sheet switch, where the tracer stitches the
surface to a neighbouring wrap, silently corrupts flattening and ink detection, and
topology checks cannot see it because the mesh stays manifold. repair.py erases the
seam and writes the corrected surface back as tifxyz with the original grid
coordinates, so it drops straight into existing flattening and ink-detection tools.
It turns "a human must inspect every segment" into "96 of 322 segments were flagged
and automatically cleaned, and here is the measured effect".

(3) What it enables that was not possible before:
- Automatic seam repair measured on the whole corpus, with a control:
    area kept: median 99.97% (flagged), 100.00% (control)
    distance verdict: 335 improved, 0 worsened
    winding verdict, a signal the repair never uses: 109 improved, 0 worsened;
      median worst winding ratio 20.1x -> 8.2x
    control: all 185 clean representations returned cell-for-cell unchanged
- A corpus-wide continuity baseline: median neighbour step is 20 voxels for 1,131
  of 1,246 representations, so one relative threshold works on every scroll with no
  tuning.
- Automatic winding numbers per segment (Open Problem #6).
- Ready-to-use results: results_seamcheck.csv (1,246 rows), results_windcheck.csv
  (697), results_repair.csv (627), and the repaired v14 surface as tifxyz.

(4) Evidence:
- 20230702185753_v14: a 3,931-voxel jump where a normal step is 20. Repair erases
  15,474 cells (0.3%) along the seam; max step goes 195x -> 5.0x, and the winding
  ratio 71.4x -> 24.1x. Before/after figure: repair_v14.png. The repaired file
  re-reads as valid tifxyz and passes seamcheck.
- Verification uses an independent signal (winding) applied unchanged before,
  after and to controls. We did not retune it after seeing results; its residual
  flags on v14 sit near the scroll core on cells with normal 3D steps (median 20.1
  voxels), which we document as a known limit of the angular measure.
- Limits stated, not hidden: repair removes the seam but does not re-trace across
  it; it never splits a surface in two (on v14 that is correct: the "tongue" is part
  of the same spiral, stitched to the wrong row); two z_dbg_gen representations got
  slightly worse on winding (19.1x -> 22.6x).
- Our own errors are documented in the repo: a retracted "provenance effect" that
  was a verdict-ordering bug, a stale CSV we regenerated, and a size filter that
  dropped a 315-cell clean representation, found by the control and fixed.

Everything reproduces with the included scripts (repairall.py, repairsummary.py).
License: MIT-0.
```
